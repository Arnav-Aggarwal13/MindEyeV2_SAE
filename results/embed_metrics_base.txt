HOW RETRIEVAL IS COMPUTED
Embeddings are flattened (425,984 numbers) and L2-normalized. S[i, j] = cosine(brain embedding i, image embedding j); the correct match is
the diagonal.
  brain_to_image: for brain embedding i, is image i the highest-scoring image?  (argmax over each row)
  image_to_brain: for image j, is brain embedding j the highest-scoring brain embedding?  (argmax over each column)
Two protocols: "all-1000" = every brain embedding vs all 1000 images (chance 0.1 %), with bootstrap 95 % CIs over images;
"300x30" = upstream's protocol, 30 random draws of 300 images (chance 0.33 %), mean top-1 with a normal 95 % CI over the 30 draws
(optimistic, the draws overlap). "centered" = each side first minus its own per-token mean over the 1000 images (not in upstream).
Mean rank is 1-based (1 = always first).

READING THE RESULTS
1. Sanity check passed: our clip_voxels retrieval reproduces upstream's protocol. Upstream's notebook output (authors' run) is 0.9996 and
   0.9987 in its two directions; ours are 0.9998 (image_to_brain) and 0.9991 (brain_to_image). The pairing of directions matches the
   "upstream labels are swapped" note, but both numbers are so close to 1 that this is only weak evidence for it.
2. prior_out is far less retrievable than clip_voxels (79.6 % vs 99.7 % brain_to_image among 1000), as expected: it is a single noisy
   diffusion sample trained to regress clip_target, while clip_voxels is trained contrastively. It is still strongly image-specific
   (chance 0.1 %; top-5 94 %).
3. Direction matters: image_to_brain is higher than brain_to_image for prior_out (85.9 % vs 79.6 % top-1). Likely because a brain embedding
   that happens to lie close to many images has to win against them all in one direction but not the other (hubness); not tested further.
4. Centering helps retrieval a little for prior_out (79.6 -> 82.6 % brain_to_image) and exposes that much of the raw cosine is a shared
   component: non-matching images score 0.276 raw but 0.000 centered, and the matching cosine drops from 0.417 to 0.196. The
   image-specific similarity is therefore about 0.2.
5. prior_out is not close to clip_target in the squared-error sense: SSE is 1.59 x the variance of the target, i.e. worse than predicting the
   per-token mean embedding. This is probably because prior_out is one noisy sample. This is NOT directly comparable to an SAE's
   reconstruction nmse (SAE(x) vs x, where the same x is compared); do not mix the two.
6. The "paired differences vs prior_out" section in base.json compares clip_voxels with prior_out and is not meaningful; it exists for later
   SAE conditions (sae_pre(prior_out), sae_ft(prior_out)).
